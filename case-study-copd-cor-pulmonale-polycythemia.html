Case study · Pulmonology · Cardiology · Hematology

COPD, a failing right heart, and blood that's too thick.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, GOLD stage 3, with pulmonary hypertension (pulmonary artery systolic pressure 58 mmHg), a dilated right ventricle with TAPSE 1.4 cm, leg swelling and an elevated BNP. Low oxygen at night and secondary polycythemia. JAK2 mutation status not yet established.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • This is cor pulmonale, driven by two causes that feed each other. The research reads the right heart failure as the meeting point of the lungs and the blood.
  • The first cause is the COPD. Long-running low oxygen tightens the small arteries of the lung, and over time that tightening turns into fixed remodeling. The pressure rises and the right ventricle works harder against it.
  • The second cause is the high red cell count, and its source is the open question. Low oxygen drives the kidneys to make more erythropoietin, which can explain the polycythemia. But it could also be polycythemia vera, a JAK2-driven blood disorder. Thicker blood strains vessels that are already remodeled, and the research links JAK2 clonal disease to chronic clots in the lungs (CTEPH). The research calls the JAK2 question the most important gap in the workup.
  • The pressure runs higher than the COPD alone would explain. That mismatch is why a second cause is on the table. The research names two more gaps: no study gives a threshold linking COPD severity to this level of lung pressure, and none shows whether right heart function recovers at this stage.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • Serum erythropoietin with JAK2 V617F testing, the first-line way to tell polycythemia vera from secondary polycythemia.
  • A V/Q scan to screen for chronic clots, then CT pulmonary angiography and right heart catheterization to confirm.
  • Hematology input, since the answer changes anticoagulation, whether to treat the blood count itself, and how urgent referral is.
