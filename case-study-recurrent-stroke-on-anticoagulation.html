Case study · Neurology · Hematology · Oncology

Two strokes in five months, on a blood thinner that was working.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Atrial fibrillation, persistent, on apixaban 5 mg twice daily for 3 years, now with two ischemic strokes in 5 months (left MCA branch, then right cerebellar) despite confirmed anticoagulant adherence.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • The research points to lung adenocarcinoma as the culprit. It drives a hypercoagulable state through tissue factor, mucin-mediated platelet aggregation and neutrophil extracellular traps, producing platelet-rich thrombi that factor Xa inhibition alone does not reliably suppress. A three-territory MRI pattern is reported to be six times more likely in malignancy-related stroke than in AF-related stroke.
  • Non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis is a live suspect. In a 115-patient series of cancer-associated NBTE, lung adenocarcinoma predominated and stroke occurred in over 80%. TEE can miss small vegetations, which is why a repeat TEE is supported.
  • Low-grade DIC or cancer-associated thrombotic microangiopathy fits the labs: schistocytes, haptoglobin below 10, falling fibrinogen and progressive thrombocytopenia.
  • One result is the branch point. ADAMTS13 activity is not yet in the record, and it separates acquired TTP from cancer-driven DIC or TMA, where treatment differs fundamentally. Antiphospholipid serology is also pending. AF contributes to overall stroke risk, but the research does not establish it as the cause of these recurrent multi-territory strokes.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • ADAMTS13 activity, to separate acquired TTP from cancer-driven DIC or TMA.
  • Repeat TEE with attention to valve leaflet morphology, to evaluate for NBTE not excluded by the prior study.
