Case study · Endocrinology · Gastroenterology

The TSH keeps climbing, no matter how high the dose goes.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Hashimoto's hypothyroidism, 9-year history, with persistent biochemical hypothyroidism (TSH 14.2 mIU/L, free T4 0.7 ng/dL) despite levothyroxine escalation from 112 to 200 mcg daily over the past 8 months.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • Three suspects, and no single proven cause. The levothyroxine is taken in one morning handful with omeprazole, ferrous sulfate and calcium carbonate. That stack is well supported as a cause of refractory hypothyroidism: acid suppression slows tablet dissolution, and iron and calcium bind the drug.
  • Separating the doses may not be enough. The research shows iron-levothyroxine chelation can persist despite dose separation, and switching to a liquid or softgel formulation has resolved refractory TSH in similar cases.
  • What the research cannot settle is whether the stack is the whole story. Oral iron failing for ten months, weight going the wrong way, worsening diarrhea, osteopenia, low vitamin D, low folate, low magnesium, mouth ulcers, angular cheilitis and an elbow rash never examined in a flare: multiple independent sources associate that constellation with undiagnosed celiac disease.
  • If celiac is confirmed, it reframes the case. Celiac is reported in 2 to 5% of autoimmune thyroid patients, shares HLA genetics with Hashimoto's, and impairs absorption of levothyroxine, iron, folate, vitamin D and B12. The interaction stack becomes secondary, and a gluten-free diet becomes the primary intervention.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • A supervised levothyroxine absorption test, to separate tablet absorption failure from other causes.
  • Anti-tTG IgA with total IgA and duodenal biopsies, to evaluate for celiac disease.
  • GAD antibodies and C-peptide, for an atypical progression from diet-controlled to metformin-requiring diabetes within a year.
