Case study · Cardiology · Nephrology

More congested than last time. The NT-proBNP says the opposite.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Acute on chronic systolic heart failure (HFrEF), decompensated, on sacubitril/valsartan. CKD stage 3b (eGFR 38, creatinine 2.2 against a baseline of 1.9), obesity (BMI 34.6) and sleep apnea. A month of over-the-counter ibuprofen, several times a week. NT-proBNP 2,100 pg/mL, down from 7,400 at his last admission, despite greater volume overload. ED BNP 410 pg/mL.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • This is real volume overload with a muted biomarker, not a false alarm. The research reads the low NT-proBNP as a signal bent by at least three forces at once, not as a sign that the fluid has some other cause.
  • The likeliest trigger is the ibuprofen. Multiple independent sources show NSAIDs cut renal prostaglandins, lower filtration and drive sodium retention, an effect amplified in kidney disease. One large epidemiologic source links NSAID use in diuretic-dependent patients to more congestive decompensation. The creatinine rise from 1.9 to 2.2 fits that, rather than a new kidney injury.
  • Two more forces push NT-proBNP down. Obesity is an established downward modifier of natriuretic peptides; the research flags that the strongest data come from HFpEF cohorts, so the effect is probable here but not precisely quantified. Sacubitril/valsartan raises BNP while NT-proBNP falls, which is why the ED BNP of 410 can't be read and NT-proBNP is the marker to follow.
  • Kidney disease should push NT-proBNP up, not down. With an eGFR of 38 the level would normally run high, so a lower value than last admission, with more congestion, means the suppressing forces are winning. Missed CPAP is a plausible extra contributor, but the research finds it weakly supported.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • Stopping the ibuprofen and IV loop diuresis, the steps the research supports as mechanistically sound.
  • Urine sodium during IV loop diuresis, to measure diuretic response; here it tracks decongestion better than serial NT-proBNP.
  • CPAP adherence, if not yet assessed this admission, to weigh right-sided congestion as a contributing cause.
