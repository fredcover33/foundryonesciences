Case study · Oncology · Endocrinology

Breast cancer, and a bone treatment quietly being undone.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in a postmenopausal woman on anastrozole, with progressive bone loss despite oral alendronate. Also on long-term omeprazole and calcium carbonate. Labs show elevated PTH and low 25-OH vitamin D.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • Three forces, not one. The research reads the bone loss and the apparent alendronate failure as three causes converging.
  • The first is the anastrozole. Multiple independent sources confirm that aromatase inhibitors drive rapid, sustained bone loss in postmenopausal women with hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer.
  • The second is the omeprazole, and it is the likeliest reason the alendronate is underperforming. Acid suppression impairs dissolution and absorption of calcium carbonate, the form she takes, lowering calcium and triggering secondary hyperparathyroidism. A clinical trial measuring omeprazole's effect on calcium absorption in postmenopausal women, and a population study linking acid-suppressing drugs to fracture, point the same way.
  • The third is the hyperparathyroidism itself. High PTH drives RANKL, which keeps recruiting new osteoclasts faster than an oral bisphosphonate can suppress them.
  • A signal to weigh, not a conclusion. One animal study links aromatase-inhibitor bone loss to cancer progression in bone, but in ER-negative disease, so its bearing on her HR-positive cancer is not established. The research also names a gap: no study yet shows whether switching the calcium form restores the bisphosphonate's effect.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • Evaluating a switch from calcium carbonate to an acid-independent calcium salt.
  • Working up the secondary hyperparathyroidism, and considering RANKL-targeted therapy as an alternative or adjunct to the bisphosphonate.
  • Serial DEXA with trabecular bone score.
