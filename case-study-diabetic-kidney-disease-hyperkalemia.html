Case study · Nephrology · Endocrinology · Cardiology

The potassium keeps climbing. No single drug is to blame.

Illustrative case · built from a test case · no real patient
Type 2 diabetes for 14 years with diabetic kidney disease, CKD stage 3b (eGFR 38). Stable heart failure on lisinopril, spironolactone, carvedilol and empagliflozin. Potassium rising across four draws over five months to 5.9 mmol/L, faster in the last six weeks, with peaked T waves on the ECG. Naproxen most days for six weeks, a completed course of TMP-SMX, and a potassium-chloride salt substitute started two months ago. Bicarbonate 19.
What the research surfaced

Read across the specialties, in one place.

  • Not one cause. A stack of them. The research reads this as several mechanisms at once, all landing on a kidney that already can't excrete potassium well. Diabetic kidney disease at stage 3b makes too little renin, so aldosterone runs low before any drug is added. A large cohort of kidney patients on these drugs shows high potassium becomes more common as kidney function falls.
  • Lisinopril and spironolactone are suspects for the slower rise. Lisinopril lowers aldosterone, and spironolactone blocks it at the kidney. On a background where aldosterone is already low, the research shows that combination can push potassium disproportionately high. Neither drug alone reaches culprit standing.
  • The steeper climb lines up with three new exposures. Naproxen suppresses the same aldosterone axis. Trimethoprim, in the TMP-SMX, blocks potassium excretion the way amiloride does, adding a third potassium-sparing drug. The salt substitute adds a potassium load of its own. All three had the means and the timing. None is proven on its own, and no potassium was checked during the antibiotic course.
  • Two drugs read differently than you might expect. Carvedilol can raise potassium, but its dose hasn't changed in 18 months, so the research doesn't establish it as a cause of the new rise. Empagliflozin works the other way and lowers potassium, so 5.9 may be the floor, not the ceiling. The bicarbonate of 19 fits the type 4 renal tubular acidosis pattern.
The next steps the research supports

What would settle it.

  • Potassium checks during and at the end of any future course of a trimethoprim antibiotic.
  • Orthostatic vital signs, since the BUN to creatinine ratio is up (about 22 to 1 against a usual 15 to 17): naproxen fits that, but low volume can't be ruled out without them.
  • Measuring how much potassium the salt substitute is adding, to separate the diet from the drugs.
